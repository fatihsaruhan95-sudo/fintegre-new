How to measure trade-in value from quote to settlement

A trade-in has more than one value. This guide explains the difference between quote, credit, payout and buyer settlement, and what to record so you can see where value changes.

A trade-in has more than one value. The customer sees a quote, receives credit or a payout, and a buyer later settles for the device. To measure a program, track each of these stages separately and record why they differ.

Four values, four moments

Each value belongs to a different moment in the journey and is agreed with a different party.

  • Quote: the value shown to the customer before the device is assessed, based on the declared model, storage and condition.
  • Credit: the value applied towards the customer’s next purchase after condition assessment, under the program’s terms.
  • Payout: where the program pays the customer money instead of credit, the amount paid and how it is paid.
  • Buyer settlement: the amount a buyer pays for the device once it has been received and checked, under the agreed buyer terms.

These values are related, but they are not the same number. Reporting them as one figure hides where value changes and who carries the difference.

In some programs the customer can choose between credit and a payout; in others only one is offered. Record which applies to each trade-in, because the two can carry different terms and costs.

Where value changes

Value usually moves at three points. Between quote and credit, when the assessed condition differs from what the customer declared. Between credit and buyer settlement, when the buyer’s check finds a different condition, or when the offer has expired before the device arrives. And over time, as market values for a model change between the quote date and the handover date.

Each change has a cause you can record: a condition difference, a timing difference or a pricing difference. Recording the cause is what makes the number useful.

What to record for each trade-in

  • Model, storage and colour where relevant.
  • Declared condition and assessed condition.
  • Quote value and date.
  • Credit or payout value and date.
  • The buyer offer used and how long it was valid.
  • Handover date and channel.
  • Buyer settlement value and date.
  • The reason for any adjustment.

Much of this data often sits in separate systems. The work is to link it to one trade-in record, so the stages can be compared.

Review the measures by channel, store and device category. A difference that looks small across the whole program can be concentrated in one store, one model or one buyer.

Measures that support decisions

  • Quote-to-credit difference: shows how closely declared condition matches assessed condition, and whether assessment guidance is clear.
  • Credit-to-settlement difference: shows whether the value given to customers is recovered from buyers, and where the program absorbs a gap.
  • Completion by stage: shows where customers leave the journey, for example after the quote or after assessment.
  • Time between stages: shows whether offer validity matches how long handover and logistics actually take.

Common measurement mistakes

Comparing quotes across programs without checking the condition mix. Reporting credit as if it were the amount recovered from buyers. Leaving out offers that expired before handover. Mixing values with and without tax. Each of these makes a program look better or worse than it is.

Getting started

Start with one channel and one device category. Agree the definitions of quote, credit, payout and buyer settlement with finance, operations and the buyer side. Then link the records and review the differences regularly. The aim is not a single headline number, but a clear view of where value is created, kept or lost.

Agree who owns each measure and how often it is reviewed. When a measure moves, the recorded reasons should point to an action: clearer condition guidance, a shorter offer window or a review of buyer terms.
