Trade-in values across Europe held steadier this quarter than last. The exception was the month after the new iPhone launch, when every older model lost value at once. Where three or more buyers bid on the same device, retailers and operators passed more of that value to their customers.

Values fall fastest in the first month

Between 9 September and 9 October, the median bid for an iPhone 15 Pro 256 GB fell 9 % across the five markets. Latvia held best at €612, helped by two buyers who export to Nordic refurbishers. Poland and Lithuania followed within €30.

The drop is not spread evenly. Models two generations old lost 11 %, while the newest flagship lost 6 %. Programs that priced from a fixed list in September paid customers less than the market offered, and the gap widened each week the list stayed unchanged.

“We used to update prices once a month. Now the buyers do it for us, every hour, and our customers see it at the counter.”

Head of trade-in, a Baltic electronics retailer

Battery health now sets the price

Battery health became the single largest price factor after screen condition. Devices under 85 % battery health received a median bid €46 lower than the same model above 90 %. Buyers now replace batteries in-house and price the cost in at the first check.

Retailers that show battery health on the customer receipt saw fewer disputes. The customer sees the same number the buyer priced, and the conversation at the counter gets shorter.

Poland recovered more slowly after the launch week, because fewer buyers compete there for older models. Programs that added a second buyer in Poland in August closed half of that gap by the end of the quarter.

Method · Median of accepted bids for devices checked through Fintegre between 1 July and 30 September 2026. Five markets: Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia, Poland, Czechia. Values exclude VAT.
